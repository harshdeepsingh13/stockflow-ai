# Shutterstock Metadata Best Practices (Condensed)

Use this as a quick reference for generating submission-ready metadata.

## Core Requirements

- Description/title language: English (except accepted proper nouns, Latin names, and common foreign terms).
- Description/title length: hard max 2048 chars.
- Project target length for description: 1000 to 1900 chars.
- Keywords language: English (same exceptions as above).
- Keywords count: minimum 25, maximum 45.
- Categories: 1 required, second optional.
- Location: optional unless editorial context requires it.

## Description/Title Rules

- Write a factual sentence or phrase that clearly describes the content.
- Cover who/what/when/where/why when relevant.
- Keep it specific and unique to the asset.
- Do not submit keyword lists as the title.
- Avoid repetition, spelling errors, grammar errors, and spam.
- Do not include links, camera settings, or irrelevant technical details.
- Commercial content: avoid trademarks, brand names, and product names.

### Editorial Additions

For documentary editorial submissions, include:

- Capture date (day/month/year; older content may allow year or decade).
- Geographic location shown in the content.

## Keyword Rules

- Use precise, relevant keywords tied directly to visible subject matter.
- Mix concrete terms and useful conceptual terms (mood, theme, use case).
- Prefer unique terms over near-duplicate forms.
- Do not include unrelated keywords (no keyword stuffing).
- Do not repeat singular/plural/variant spam chains.
- Do not include links, case numbers, or irrelevant trademark terms.

## Compliance and Safety

- Metadata must accurately match the depicted subject matter.
- Use respectful, accurate identity language.
- Prohibited: hateful, racist, derogatory, discriminatory, or disrespectful language.
- Never include personally identifiable or sensitive financial data.
- No emojis in titles/keywords.

## Common Rejection Triggers

- Metadata unrelated to the visual content.
- Editorial content missing or misreporting date/location.
- Trademark use in commercial metadata.
- Repetitive or spammy keyword patterns.
- Extremely vague titles.

## Quick Good vs Bad

- Good title: "Aerial view of Paris at sunset with the Eiffel Tower and city skyline."
- Bad title: "Paris sunset tower city travel beautiful."
- Good keywords: relevant, varied, specific terms describing subject, style, and context.
- Bad keywords: repeated variants and unrelated terms.
